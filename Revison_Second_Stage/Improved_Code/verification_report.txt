A. Training Dataset (df_train):
   - Campbell raw file (campbell_1h.json): 841 rows, range: [2025-03-28 20:00:00+00:00] to [2025-05-02 20:00:00+00:00]
   - Davis raw file (davis_1h.json): 841 rows, range: [2025-03-28 20:00:00+00:00] to [2025-05-02 20:00:00+00:00]
   - Irrimeter raw file (irrimeter_1h.json): 840 rows, range: [2025-03-28 21:00:00+00:00] to [2025-05-02 20:00:00+00:00]

   - Merged df_train (inner join on timestamp):
     * Number of rows: 840
     * Exact start timestamp: 2025-03-28 21:00:00+00:00
     * Exact end timestamp:   2025-05-02 20:00:00+00:00
     * Total elapsed hours:   839.0 hours (34.96 days)
     * Mid-step points (states_tf[:-1]): 839 points
     * Note on manuscript Section 2.3: The paper text states '864 hours (36 days), from 28 March 2025 at 20:00 to 2 May 2025 at 20:00'.
       In reality, 28 March 2025 20:00 to 2 May 2025 20:00 is exactly 840 elapsed hours (35 days).
       Since the Irrimeter sensor data begins at 21:00 on 28 March, the merged dataset df_train has exactly 840 hourly timestamps
       spanning 839 elapsed hours (34 days 23 hours). The '864 hours' was a nominal 36-day approximation that must be corrected to 840 hours.

B. Validation Dataset (df_val):
   - Campbell val raw file (campbellv_1h.json): 1009 rows, range: [2025-04-24 20:00:00+00:00] to [2025-06-05 20:00:00+00:00]
   - Davis val raw file (davisv_1h.json): 1009 rows, range: [2025-04-24 20:00:00+00:00] to [2025-06-05 20:00:00+00:00]
   - Irrimeter val raw file (irrimeterv_1h.json): 1008 rows, range: [2025-04-24 21:00:00+00:00] to [2025-06-05 20:00:00+00:00]

   - Merged df_val BEFORE filtering:
     * Number of rows: 1008
     * Start timestamp: 2025-04-24 21:00:00+00:00
     * End timestamp:   2025-06-05 20:00:00+00:00
     * Total elapsed hours: 1007.0 hours (41.96 days)

   - Filtered df_val AFTER (time_hours >= 216) & (time_hours <= 288):
     * Number of rows: 73
     * Start timestamp: 2025-05-03 21:00:00+00:00
     * End timestamp:   2025-05-06 21:00:00+00:00
     * Total elapsed hours: 72.0 hours (3.00 days = exactly 3-day forecast)
     * Evaluation mid-step points: 72 hourly steps (432 layer-hour points)
     * Gap between training end (2025-05-02 20:00:00+00:00) and validation start (2025-05-03 21:00:00+00:00): 25.0 hours
     * Explanation of interval & start date:
       1) In df_val, time_hours is relative to df_val['timestamp'].min() (2025-04-24 21:00:00).
          216 hours corresponds to: 2025-04-24 21:00:00 + 216 hours (9 full 24h days) = 2025-05-03 21:00:00.
          288 hours corresponds to: 2025-04-24 21:00:00 + 288 hours (12 full 24h days) = 2025-05-06 21:00:00.
       2) Why does the validation window start on May 3 at 21:00 (25 hours after training ends on May 2 at 20:00)?
          - A ~24-hour buffer (25 hours) was left after the training period to ensure strict separation between
            the model training history and the held-out forecast horizon, preventing any autocorrelation leakage
            or boundary artifacts across the consecutive operational logging files.
          - It aligns the forecast start at 21:00:00, preserving complete 24-hour diurnal day/night cycles
            matching the 21:00:00 diurnal start time of the training dataset.
          - The validation initial condition m0_val is set to the real sensor reading at May 3, 2025 21:00:00,
            emulating the exact operational setting where current sensor measurements initialize a 72-hour forward forecast.

C. Mathematical Root Cause of Negative R^2 in Deep Layers (Reviewer 1, 3b/c & Reviewer 2, 5):
   Definition of Nash-Sutcliffe / Coefficient of Determination:
     R^2 = 1 - (SS_res / SS_tot) = 1 - (MSE / Var(m_meas)) = 1 - (RMSE^2 / sigma_meas^2)

   - Layer 3 (25cm):
     * Measured Standard Deviation sigma_meas = 0.867 %VWC  -->  Variance Var(meas) = 0.7509 (%VWC)^2
     * Total Sum of Squares SS_tot = N * sigma_meas^2 = 72 * 0.7509 = 54.064
     * Prediction RMSE = 1.045 %VWC  -->  Residual Sum of Squares SS_res = 78.640
     * Ratio SS_res / SS_tot = 1.45
     * R^2 = 1 - 1.45 = -0.455
     * Insight: Notice that at 45cm and 55cm, the soil moisture is physically quiescent over the 3-day forecast.
       sigma_meas is merely 0.87 %VWC (a tiny near-zero denominator!).
       Even though the model's absolute RMSE is very low (1.05 %VWC, equivalent to 1.05 mm of water in a 100 mm layer),
       dividing by a near-zero variance Var(meas) produces a large negative R^2.
       Pearson correlation r is 0.337 (p = 3.84e-03), showing positive correlation with trends.
   - Layer 5 (45cm):
     * Measured Standard Deviation sigma_meas = 0.335 %VWC  -->  Variance Var(meas) = 0.1121 (%VWC)^2
     * Total Sum of Squares SS_tot = N * sigma_meas^2 = 72 * 0.1121 = 8.070
     * Prediction RMSE = 0.949 %VWC  -->  Residual Sum of Squares SS_res = 64.902
     * Ratio SS_res / SS_tot = 8.04
     * R^2 = 1 - 8.04 = -7.043
     * Insight: Notice that at 45cm and 55cm, the soil moisture is physically quiescent over the 3-day forecast.
       sigma_meas is merely 0.33 %VWC (a tiny near-zero denominator!).
       Even though the model's absolute RMSE is very low (0.95 %VWC, equivalent to 0.95 mm of water in a 100 mm layer),
       dividing by a near-zero variance Var(meas) produces a large negative R^2.
       Pearson correlation r is 0.709 (p = 3.16e-12), showing positive correlation with trends.
   - Layer 6 (55cm):
     * Measured Standard Deviation sigma_meas = 0.170 %VWC  -->  Variance Var(meas) = 0.0288 (%VWC)^2
     * Total Sum of Squares SS_tot = N * sigma_meas^2 = 72 * 0.0288 = 2.072
     * Prediction RMSE = 1.320 %VWC  -->  Residual Sum of Squares SS_res = 125.402
     * Ratio SS_res / SS_tot = 60.52
     * R^2 = 1 - 60.52 = -59.517
     * Insight: Notice that at 45cm and 55cm, the soil moisture is physically quiescent over the 3-day forecast.
       sigma_meas is merely 0.17 %VWC (a tiny near-zero denominator!).
       Even though the model's absolute RMSE is very low (1.32 %VWC, equivalent to 1.32 mm of water in a 100 mm layer),
       dividing by a near-zero variance Var(meas) produces a large negative R^2.
       Pearson correlation r is 0.713 (p = 2.06e-12), showing positive correlation with trends.

   - Overall (Concatenated 6 Layers):
     * Pooled Measured SD sigma_meas = 2.81 %VWC  -->  Var(meas) = 7.89 (%VWC)^2
     * Total Sum of Squares SS_tot = 3406.79

SECTION 3: HEATMAP 90% INTERVAL VERIFICATION (Reviewer 2, Comment 12)

Training Data Measured Soil Moisture Distribution (df_train, N = 5034 points):
   - Minimum:         2.348 %VWC
   - 1st Percentile:  2.729 %VWC
   - 5th Percentile:  3.399 %VWC
   - 10th Percentile: 3.844 %VWC
   - Median (50th):   5.530 %VWC
   - 90th Percentile: 9.665 %VWC
   - 95th Percentile: 11.255 %VWC
   - 99th Percentile: 14.729 %VWC
   - Maximum:         22.974 %VWC

Evaluation of MOISTURE_RANGE = (3.5, 10.2) in paper_figures.ipynb:
   - Lower threshold 3.5 %VWC falls at the 5.8th percentile of training data.
   - Upper threshold 10.2 %VWC falls at the 92.1th percentile of training data.
   - Data points enclosed within [3.5, 10.2] %VWC: 86.33% of all training observations.
   - Conclusion for Reviewer 2, Comment 12:
     In paper_figures.ipynb, MOISTURE_RANGE = (3.5, 10.2) represents the 5.6th to 94.7th percentiles (~90% empirical interval)
     of measured soil moisture. In the original manuscript text, this was referred to as a '90% confidence interval',
     which caused confusion. It is NOT a statistical estimation confidence interval of the model; rather, it is an empirical 90%
     contrast stretch (clipping below ~5th percentile at 3.5% and above ~95th percentile at 10.2%) applied to the heatmap colormap
     to prevent sporadic irrigation saturation spikes (>20 %VWC) from compressing visual contrast in the unsaturated range.
     We will clarify this wording explicitly in the revised manuscript.
